Hana Bauta

I’m drawn to public health because I believe meaningful change begins with understanding not only what affects people’s health, but also who is being left out of the solutions.

My interest in the field grew from seeing how gaps in access, communication, and resources can prevent even the most promising research and interventions from reaching the people who need them. In 2018, I experienced this firsthand while helping adapt child mental health questionnaires into picture-based formats for families in Uganda and Nepal. It taught me that improving health outcomes requires more than developing effective tools; it requires making them accessible, relevant, and responsive to the communities they serve.

I earned my B.S. in Public Health from Rutgers University, graduating magna cum laude, and am now pursuing an MPH at Cornell University with a concentration in Infectious Disease Epidemiology. I chose this concentration to deepen my understanding of disease transmission and outbreak dynamics, while strengthening the epidemiological and biostatistical methods needed to investigate complex public health problems. Through my research experiences with NYU Langone’s Department of Population Health, I have also explored how data can inform more equitable health interventions, including conducting a national needs assessment of adolescent access to mental health care and developing an onboarding toolkit for a digital intervention.

Across my experiences, I have become particularly interested in the intersection of infectious disease epidemiology, health equity, and applied research. Whether studying disease patterns, evaluating surveillance systems, or designing interventions, I want to understand not only what the data reveals, but whose experiences they represent and how findings can translate into meaningful action.

I see my MPH as an important foundation for eventually pursuing a PhD, where I can further develop the methodological expertise to investigate disease dynamics, address gaps in public health evidence, and contribute to research that informs policy and practice. Ultimately, I want my work to bridge rigorous epidemiological research and real-world public health needs, helping turn what we learn into solutions that make a difference.

APEx · Deliverable 1 NYU Langone Health R · complex survey design

Who isn’t getting care?

A needs assessment of U.S. teens’ access to mental health care, using the 2024 National Survey of Children’s Health (21,960 teens ages 12 to 17). I compared low-income teens (at or below 200% of the poverty level) with higher-income teens to show where unmet need is concentrated and what the team’s digital tool had to design around.

Purpose & impact

The brief gave the product and evaluation teams a shared, population-representative picture of unmet need—so design choices (who the tool is for, what not to require at first use) were grounded in data rather than assumptions.

My role

Designed and ran the analysis; built a reproducible R Markdown pipeline with weighted prevalence tables and figures; delivered the needs-assessment brief.

APEx · Deliverable 2 NYU Langone Health Content design

Equity-informed onboarding toolkit for an adolescent digital mental health intervention

An adolescent-facing onboarding toolkit for a proposed digital mental health intervention, built from the needs assessment to lower barriers to care for underserved youth. The five-screen mobile flow uses supportive, non-diagnostic language, explains data use and privacy, replaces diagnostic labels with an experience-based check-in and an RDoC-informed explore step, and offers a brief, low-burden activity for low motivation.

Purpose & impact

Translates findings on unmet need and access barriers into intervention design, specifying what adolescents see, in what order, and what the tool should never require before engagement (such as insurance information or a diagnostic label). Includes plain-language materials, safety, privacy, and equity guardrails, and handoff notes for evaluation and implementation. It is a design prototype and has not been clinically validated.

From engagement metrics to precision care

A co-authored poster on using computational phenotyping to personalize digital mental health tools. Clustering 181 adults by age, tech use, and distress turned up four distinct phenotypes, including highly engaged users with the highest distress, which shows that high engagement doesn’t mean low distress.

VTPEH 6182 · Public Health Planning Spring 2026 Group grant proposal

TickSafe Finger Lakes Initiative

A group grant proposal for a one-year pilot to reduce tick-borne disease in the Finger Lakes through hands-on community outreach. Grounded in Social Cognitive Theory, the initiative focused on strengthening tick-borne disease knowledge, increasing the visibility of prevention messaging, and building the practical skills needed to adopt consistent protective behaviors.

Purpose & impact

Move beyond passive information toward active outreach in high-exposure settings, with sustainability and equity built into the plan—and a model that could be adapted and replicated as tick populations and disease risk expand.

VTPEH 6270 · Data Analysis with R May 2026

Internet use and anxiety among U.S. college students

An analysis of the relationship between internet use and anxiety among 34,953 U.S. college students using 2024–2025 Healthy Minds Study data. Students who spent more time online reported higher anxiety, but this relationship became much weaker after accounting for depression and other factors, with differences among sub-group populations, such as first generation students.

Purpose & impact

Understanding which students may be more vulnerable to anxiety can help campus mental health teams develop more targeted support. The accompanying report and interactive Shiny app allow users to explore the findings.
